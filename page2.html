Where to go

Old Faithful

Old Faithful is the world's most famous geyser, located in Yellowstone National Park's Upper Geyser Basin, known for its predictable eruptions. It typically erupts every 50-127 minutes, for 1.5 to 5 minutes, and expels 3,700-8,400 gallons of boiling water. The geyser is influenced by underground geology, heat from the Yellowstone Caldera, and can be affected by events like earthquakes or human interference.

Thumb Geyser

Its name comes from the thumb-like shape of the lake's projection. The basin features hot springs, mud pots, and geysers along a boardwalk trail, with notable features including the deep Abyss Pool and the Fishing Cone.

Mammoth Hot Springs

Mammoth Hot Springs is a unique geothermal feature in Yellowstone National Park, located near the North Entrance, known for its extensive travertine terraces formed by mineral-rich hot springs. Visitors can walk along boardwalks to see the constantly changing landscape, which is created as hot water emerges from the ground and deposits calcium carbonate. Swimming is not permitted in the springs, but it is possible to swim in areas like the Boiling River, which are fed by the thermal runoff.

Tower Falls

Tower Fall is a waterfall on Tower Creek in the northeastern region of Yellowstone National Park, in the U.S. state of Wyoming. Approximately 1,000 yards (910 m) upstream from the creek's confluence with the Yellowstone River, the fall plunges 132 feet (40 m). Its name comes from the rock pinnacles at the top of the fall. Tower Creek and Tower Fall are located approximately three miles south of Roosevelt Junction on the Tower-Canyon road.

Lamar River

Prior to the 1884 Geological Survey of the park, the Lamar was known as the East Fork of the Yellowstone River. During that survey, Geologist Arnold Hague named the river for L.Q.C. (Lucius Quintius Cincinnatus) Lamar, then Secretary of the Interior (March 1885 to January 1888), and a former slaveholder and author of the Mississippi Ordinance of Secession.
